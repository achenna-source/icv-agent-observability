# Passes set aside, kept rather than deleted

These two files are complete, well formed and accepted by `reception.py`. They are set
aside because they are not independent readings, not because anything is wrong with them.

`responses_B.jsonl` gives the same label as `responses_A.jsonl` on all fifty items, free
and computed, while sharing no sentence with it: one decision process written out twice in
different prose. See `../provenance_B.md`.

`responses_D.jsonl` shares 45 of its 50 notes word for word with `responses_C.jsonl` and
agrees with it on 48 of 50 labels: one text edited into two files. See `../provenance_D.md`.

Counting them would inflate unanimity and would claim more annotators than the round has.
The analysis therefore runs on `../responses_A.jsonl`, `../responses_C.jsonl` and
`../responses_E.jsonl`, which are three distinct readings.

They stay here so that anyone can check the claim instead of taking it on trust.
